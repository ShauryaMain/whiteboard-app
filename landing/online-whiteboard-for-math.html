graphs · shapes · ruler · compass · calculator A free online whiteboard for math

Clean Slate is an online whiteboard with real math tools built in: graphs that plot the equations you type, statistics charts, exact shapes with the angles and lengths marked, a ruler and compass, and a scientific calculator that gives exact answers. It's free, and you can try it without signing up.

Try it on a graph, no sign-in Start free

The demo opens a real board in your browser. Nothing to install, and your board is kept in your browser until you sign in.

Graphs that plot the equations you type

Open the Maths menu and choose Graph. In the Plot tab, type an equation such as y = x² − 3 with the same keys as the calculator, and the curve is drawn on the board straight away.

  • Sliders. Letters like a, b and c get sliders on the board, so you can drag them in the lesson and the class watches the curve change.
  • Key points. Mark the roots, the turning points and where two curves cross.
  • Read it off. Tap a curve to read a point, with its tangent and gradient.
  • Shade it. Shade inequalities and the area under a curve.
  • Points and tables. Plot points from a table, join them up or add a line of best fit, and put a table of values beside the graph with the y row left blank for the class to fill in.

Put a graph beside each question: a board can hold several, and each one moves, resizes, copies and undoes like anything else. Double-tap a graph to change it. In a live class, students see every change as you make it.

Statistics charts

The same Graph tool draws bar charts, histograms, scatter graphs with a line of best fit, and box plots. Type or paste the data and the axes fit themselves.

Exact shapes: polygons, triangles, circles and 3D solids

Choose Insert, then Shape. Pick a family, type the numbers you want, and a live preview shows the shape before it goes on the board.

  • Polygons from 3 to 20 sides. The number of sides, the interior angle and the exterior angle are linked: change one and the others follow.
  • Triangles: equilateral, isosceles, right-angled, or any triangle from three sides, two sides and the angle between them, or two angles and the side between them.
  • Quadrilaterals: square, rectangle, parallelogram, rhombus, trapezium (trapezoid) and kite, with the equal-side marks and parallel arrows worked out for you.
  • Circles: circle, semicircle, sector, segment, arc and ellipse.
  • 3D solids: cube, cuboid, prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone and sphere. Drag the solid to turn it, with the hidden edges dashed.

You choose what gets labelled: angles, side lengths, corner letters and equal-side marks. Under the preview are the facts for that shape, such as perimeter, area and angle sum, or volume and surface area for a solid.

Ask for something that can't exist, such as a regular polygon with a 100° interior angle or a triangle whose sides can't meet, and it tells you why.

The shape lands on the board as ordinary ink with text labels, so you can move it, copy it or rub parts out. To change its measurements afterwards, insert a new one.

Geometry tools: ruler and compass

Open the Maths menu and pick Ruler or Compass. Both sit on the board like the real things.

  • Ruler: marked in centimetres or inches, with its angle shown as you turn it (it clicks to every 15°). Write along its edge and the line comes out straight, with its length shown.
  • Compass: drag the pin to set the centre and the pencil to set the radius, then draw circles and arcs.

Graph paper, set up the way you draw it

The graph's Axes and paper tab sets it all up, with a live preview before it goes on the board:

  • A range and a scale for each axis, the origin where you want it, axis names, arrows and a title.
  • π or degrees along x, with ordinary numbers up y.
  • One tap for four quadrants, the first quadrant, the top half or a unit circle.
  • Number lines, with points and inequalities marked on them.
  • Squared, dotted, isometric, log or polar paper.
  • Your own presets, and snapping so ruler lines and points land on the grid.

Then draw on it with the pen, as you would on paper.

A scientific calculator with exact answers

The calculator is laid out like a school scientific calculator. It gives exact answers such as ½, 2√2 or π/4, and one tap switches between the exact form and a decimal. It works in degrees or radians, keeps a history of your last answers, and its Add to board button writes the calculation onto the board.

Squared, dot grid and isometric paper

Set the whole page to squared paper for graphs and tables, dot grid for diagrams, or isometric paper for 3D shapes. The pattern stays in place as you zoom and pan, and the eraser never rubs it out.

Write maths by hand, or type it

Maths is quicker to write than to type. Clean Slate is made for a stylus: the pen follows your hand, a scribble over a mistake rubs it out, and on an iPad your palm can rest on the screen.

When you'd rather type, text boxes set the maths for you: x^2 becomes x², x_2 becomes x₂ and 3/4 becomes a stacked fraction. Style any words with bold, colour, highlight, size and font.

Common questions

Can it draw accurate shapes?

Yes. Insert, then Shape draws polygons, triangles, quadrilaterals, circles and 3D solids to the sides and angles you set, and labels the measurements for you.

Can I change a shape after adding it?

It becomes ordinary ink, so you can move it, copy it and rub parts out. To change its measurements, insert a new shape.

Does it have a ruler and a compass?

Yes. Both are in the Maths menu. The ruler is marked in centimetres or inches, shows its angle, and straightens lines drawn along its edge. The compass draws circles and arcs. There is no protractor.

Can I type maths?

Yes. In a text box, x^2 becomes x², x_2 becomes x₂ and 3/4 becomes a stacked fraction as you type.

Does it have graph paper?

Yes, in two ways. The Page menu sets the whole board to squared paper, and the Graph tool places axes with the range and scale you choose for each one.

Can it plot a function for me?

Yes. Type the equation in the Graph tool's Plot tab and it's drawn on the board. Letters like a, b and c get sliders, and you can mark roots and turning points, read off gradients, and shade inequalities or the area under a curve.

Can it draw statistics charts?

Yes: bar charts, histograms, scatter graphs with a line of best fit, and box plots. Type or paste the data and the axes fit themselves.

Is the calculator a graphing calculator?

No. It is a scientific calculator with exact answers, fractions, powers, roots, logs and trig in degrees or radians. Plotting is done in the Graph tool.

Is it free?

Yes. You can also try the demo board without signing up.
