a board for every student A free online whiteboard for the classroom

With Clean Slate, every student in the room gets their own board. They join with a short code, you see every board at once as they write, and you can step in exactly where it's needed. It's free, with no time limit.

Try a pretend class, no sign-in Start free

The demo opens a real board in your browser. Nothing to install, and your board is kept in your browser until you sign in.

How a live class works

  1. Start a live class. Open any board and press Start live class. Clean Slate shows a six-character code.
  2. Students join with the code. They type it on any device, add their first name, and they're in. No account, no email address.
  3. Everyone works. Students follow your board and work on their own, with the same tools you have. On a tablet or laptop they can put the two side by side.
  4. You see the whole class. Class overview shows every student's board at once, live. Open any one to look closer.

See who is stuck before they ask

On paper you only see a student's working when you walk past. Here every board is on your screen, so you can spot the slip in line two while the student is still on line three.

Students can put a hand up when they're stuck, or say they're done. Raised hands go to the top of your class list, and you lower them once you've helped.

Mark their work as they go

Open a student's board and choose how to help:

  • Comment: tap the board to pin a numbered note exactly where you mean.
  • Write: circle the mistake or write the correction in red, green or blue. Only you can rub out what you write, and their own work is never touched.

Steer the whole class

  • Send my board puts your question on every student's board, so everyone starts from the same place. They write over it but can't rub it out.
  • Show to class puts one student's working on every screen, with their name or without it.
  • Pens down pauses every student's board and brings them all to yours.
  • Save boards downloads every student's board as a PDF, a page each, or as pictures, before you end the class.

What students need

A device with a browser. It can be an iPad, an Android tablet, a Chromebook or a laptop. They don't install anything, and they don't create an account or give an email address.

What it's built for

Clean Slate's live class is built for a normal class of 20 to 30 students working at the same time. There is no charge and no session time limit.

Students' boards have the same tools as yours, including graphs, shapes, the ruler, compass and calculator, and every paper style. Pictures, simulations and documents are added from your board only.

Teach from one worksheet together

Open a PDF beside your board and every student sees it too, with what you write on it. Point at a line with the laser pointer, and turn on Follow me so everyone's copy goes to the page you're on. On a phone, students switch between the board and the document.

Common questions

Do students need to sign in?

No. They join with a six-character class code and their first name.

Is there a time limit on a class?

No. A live class runs until you end it.

How many students can join?

It is built for classes of 20 to 30 students working at once.

Can I see every student's board at once?

Yes. Class overview shows every board side by side, live, with raised hands and students who say they're done marked on each one.

Can students see each other's boards?

Only when you choose Show to class, which puts one student's board on every screen, with or without their name. Otherwise each student sees your board and their own.

Can I write on a student's board?

Yes. Pin a numbered comment, or switch to Write and mark their work in red, green or blue. Only you can rub out what you write, and their own writing is never changed.

Is students' work saved after the class?

Not on our servers. Press Save boards before you end the class to download everyone's board as a PDF or as pictures. If a student refreshes or rejoins during the class, their work comes back. Your own board is saved.
